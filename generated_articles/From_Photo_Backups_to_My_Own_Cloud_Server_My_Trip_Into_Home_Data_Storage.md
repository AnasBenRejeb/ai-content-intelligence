I had a hunch that the Seagate would be the better buy, so I took a chance and got myself a 1TB Exos. I had heard good things about the Seagate, so I was hoping for a good deal. But the price was too steep. I ended up with a Seagate in a Seagate Exos 2.

It was a great time to be a fan of hard drives. They were cheap, but they weren’t cheap enough, and I had a feeling this was going to change. I needed a larger drive. My current setup consists of a 4TB Seagate and two 2TB Seagates (the other one is in an external enclosure).

Then, in November 2014, my Drobo died. It was a very sad day, but I had been thinking about upgrading to a SSD. I was considering a Samsung 850 EVO, but I couldn’t justify the cost. So, I settled for a 1TB Samsung 850 EVO and a 4TB Samsung 850 EVO. I was a little worried about the cost, but I figured I could afford it, so I got one.

It’s been three years, and I can safely say that the Samsung has been a great choice. I still have a 4TB SSD, but I haven’t had a chance to install it yet. It’s been a while since I installed a new SSD, so I thought I’d write a piece on the topic.

One of the biggest advantages of an SSD is the speed. A 4TB SSD can handle a lot of stuff. I can read a 150 GB movie in one hour, or I can read a 150 GB book in three hours. I can watch a 1080p Blu-ray movie in one hour and read 100 pages of text in about two hours.

But there’s more to it than just speed.

Speed isn’t everything. A great SSD is more than just an SSD. I have to say that the Samsung EVO series has been my go-to for a long time. The speed is fantastic, but there are a few other things I appreciate about an SSD.

Firstly, a solid state drive can be an excellent choice if you have a lot of apps running in the background. Apps and programs running in the background can be slowing down your system. SSDs are designed to run without any background apps running.

Secondly, SSDs are very durable. They can withstand drops, spills, and even extreme temperatures. It’s a good feeling to know that my hard drive can withstand whatever life throws at it.

Thirdly, SSDs are very affordable. In comparison, an HDD can be more expensive, but the speed difference is sometimes just enough to justify the price.

In the end, I decided to upgrade my SSD to an SSD. It wasn’t easy to find an SSD that would fit my current setup, but I eventually found a 32GB 2.5-inch SSD from Samsung. It’s a great choice for my setup, and I’m very happy with it.

I hope this article will help you understand the benefits of upgrading to an SSD. I can’t wait to see what the future holds for SSD technology.

It’s been three years since I had my Drobo and the Samsung SSD. I’m happy with both choices. I haven’t had to upgrade in a while, but I hope I never have to again. But if I do, I’m sure that I will upgrade to an SSD.
